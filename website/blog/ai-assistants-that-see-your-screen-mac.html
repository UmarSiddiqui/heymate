AI assistants that can see your Mac screen

Compare how HeyMate, ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini get screen context on Mac, then try one sample task to see which fits your workflow.

For a single screenshot, start with Claude Desktop quick entry. For a live spoken conversation with screen context, consider ChatGPT Voice. Gemini can pull in context from the app you are using. For help that points to a Mac control and waits while you try it, use HeyMate with a vision-capable provider.

Compare what you actually share

Screen awareness can mean an attachment you select, a window you share mid-conversation, or fresh captures as a task progresses. Pick the workflow you want before comparing model names or plans. Try the same question in each app

Open TextEdit and type a few lines of sample text. Bring that window to the front and use each assistant’s documented method to share it. Ask how to export it as a PDF and where to start. Apple’s TextEdit guide gives you an answer to check against.

Pay attention to whether you could choose the area shared, whether the answer referred to your actual app, and how easily you got the next step after the menu changed. If you need a pointer, look for an on-screen cue that identifies the control. If you want to keep sharing under manual control, check whether you can select each image before sending.

Check your Mac and your account

HeyMate supports Apple silicon and Intel Macs on macOS 14.2 or later. Screen questions require a vision-capable connection. Claude Code and ChatGPT through Codex use their respective CLI sign-ins. HeyMate provider setup

Google lists Apple silicon and macOS 15 or later for Gemini on Mac. Claude lists macOS 12 for quick entry and macOS 14 for quick-entry dictation. OpenAI lists eligible paid plans for desktop Voice. Check the feature documentation above before changing plans or installing a new app. Gemini for Mac requirements

Choose how much context the task needs

If you only need to identify a toolbar button, one cropped screenshot may be enough. If the interface changes after every click, refreshing context can make the next answer more relevant. Start with sample material and see what screen access can expose. When the job is learning an interface, try a voice walkthrough you can do yourself.
