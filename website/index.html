You do the clicking. It does the explaining.

Open any desktop app or website and ask “walk me through this.” Use something without private content for your first try.

  1. ask

    Say what you want to do.

    Hold ⌃⌥ and ask out loud, or type into the notch.

  2. plan

    It looks, then lays out the steps.

    HeyMate reads the screen you're on and tells you how many steps it will take.

  3. point

    The pointer shows the next click.

    One step at a time, explained in plain words while you do it.

  4. check

    It looks again before moving on.

    Click the target or say “next.” A fresh look at the screen decides the following step.

HeyMate talks. Your agents do the work.

Questions stay with HeyMate. Anything longer goes to an agent that works in its own folder, plans first, and waits for your yes.

HeyMatein the notch

The one you talk to. It sees the screen when you ask, answers out loud, and hands bigger jobs to the right agent.

  • Points while it explains. Walkthroughs for apps and websites.
  • Remembers you. Saves facts and preferences you agree to keep.
  • Quiet on calls. Mutes itself when another app is using the mic.
Agentsin the background

Named helpers for ongoing work. Each one runs on Claude Code, Codex, or OpenCode with the account you already have.

  • Plan, then ask. Nothing changes until you approve the plan.
  • Routines. “Every morning at 8” runs on its own and reports back.
  • Undo. Snapshots of local files before an approved run.

Rendered from the app with sample data.

Describe an agent in one sentence.

Your AI drafts the rest: a name, how it works, what it must ask you about first, and a first task. Change anything before you create it.

Connect an app once. Every agent can use it.

Integrations are shared across HeyMate and all your agents. Turn one off and it's off everywhere.

Know what it can see.

The screen is captured when you ask, and between walkthrough steps. Routines and approved agents can run in the background.

On your disk

Chats, memory, agents, and routines live in ~/Library/Application Support/heymate/. Chat history keeps no screenshots or audio.

Your keys stay here

Credentials are stored on this Mac and used only with the services you connect. Questions and screen context go to the AI you picked.

Skips what's private

Password managers and System Settings are never captured by default.

Hidden on calls

One switch hides the notch and pointer from screen shares.

What's new.

HeyMate updates itself. Here's what the latest releases changed.

  1. 1.0.47Oct 12, 2026
    • Make an agent from one sentence. Your own AI drafts its name, habits, what it must ask you first, and a first task. Change anything before you create it.
    • Just HeyMate and your agents. The main chat is HeyMate again, everyone else is an agent, and running work lives in Activity.
    • Integrations, shared by every agent. Connect an app once and every agent can use it.
  2. 1.0.46Oct 12, 2026
    • Safer on the web. Web pages you visit can no longer reach HeyMate's built-in local server, so a site can't make HeyMate speak, take a screenshot, or add an agent behind your back.
    • Every release is checked by hand. New versions are now approved before they're signed and published.
  3. 1.0.44Oct 12, 2026
    • Chats and Agents, side by side. The sidebar has two tabs: Chats for your conversations with HeyMate, and Agents for everyone else.
    • New face packs. Signal, Margin, and Instrument join the originals.

Install it three ways.

It asks for Microphone, Accessibility, and Screen Recording. Here's exactly why.

download

The DMG

Open it and drag HeyMate into Applications. Builds are self-signed, not notarized by Apple. If macOS blocks the first launch, use Open Anyway in Privacy & Security.

Download for Mac
homebrew

One command

Installs the latest release and keeps it in Homebrew's list.

brew install umarsiddiqui/tap/heymate

terminal

The install script

Read it first. It replaces /Applications/HeyMate.app and clears the quarantine flag.

curl -fsSL getheymate.vercel.app/install.sh | bash
